About me

Sandipsinh Rathod

Backend engineer. Rust, distributed systems, and a curiosity about how things work.

I build backend systems, mostly in Rust. I like working close to the metal: gRPC gateways, caching layers, query optimizers, and anything where latency matters.

I've been tinkering with computers since I was a kid. I started reverse-engineering Android apps at 12 and eventually moved on to building and publishing my own apps. Along the way I explored Java, built an open-source NoSQL database, and spent a lot of time hopping between Linux distributions and setting up KVM.

I'm drawn to the mathematically inclined parts of computer science. Learning about RSA, Diffie–Hellman, AES, and TLS led me toward Rust and systems programming. Competitive programming has been another part of that exploration.

What I've worked on

At Tailcall, I contributed to a GraphQL runtime: WebAssembly support, runtime abstractions, gRPC reflection, and query execution. My hackathon write-up tells the story of getting involved.

My work on ForgeCode has covered tool architecture and MCP integration, followed by agent–client integration and the macOS experience.

I also build small Rust libraries and contribute fixes to the libraries I use. The timeline collects that work, including the short notes that wouldn't otherwise become a full blog post.
